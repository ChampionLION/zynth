# 1. ZYNTH'S FUNDAMENTAL VISION

## 1.1 Product identity

Zynth is an adaptive behavioral synthesis and personal-growth platform presented through RPG-inspired progression.

Its central philosophy remains:

"Synthesize yourself. Reach your zenith."

Zynth must not become a generic educational platform, traditional habit tracker, or conventional RPG disconnected from meaningful personal growth.

The user's actions and demonstrated development remain central.

## 1.2 The expanded vision

Zynth is intended to become an infinitely expandable progression universe.

There must be no hardcoded final chapter, final region, maximum storyline, or mandatory ending to the overarching experience.

The world should be capable of expanding through:

- New quests.
- New missions.
- Additional narrative chapters.
- New story arcs.
- New regions.
- New landmarks.
- New professions and profession evolutions.
- New skill branches.
- New achievements.
- New titles.
- New cosmetic rewards.
- New character customization options.
- New environmental transformations.
- Additional story choices.
- Future worldbuilding content.

"Endless" means extensible without a predetermined terminal endpoint.

It does not mean generating infinite content immediately, storing unbounded histories in user profile records, or running expensive AI generation continuously.

Design the system to grow through modular content, stable contracts, and controlled content releases.

## 1.3 Sustainability principle

The system should reward meaningful activity without punishing users for rest, temporary absence, or changing priorities.

Previously earned world progress, achievements, cosmetic rewards, and narrative milestones should not disappear simply because a user misses a day.

Avoid coercive streak mechanics, deliberately stressful reminders, manipulative randomized rewards, and harmful engagement loops.

Retain existing gameplay functionality unless its behavior is explicitly changed by the new product requirements.

# 5. CRITICAL CORRECTION — QUESTS AND MISSIONS

## 5.1 Original blueprint behavior

The original Engine 2 specifies a mission quest type triggered through a seven-day streak condition.

It also specifies:

- Personalized quest generation.
- Multiple quest types.
- Mission difficulty differences.
- Mission steps.
- Mission-specific EXP behavior.
- Mission rewards.
- Adaptive difficulty.
- Application-controlled calculations.

Do not remove these capabilities.

## 5.2 Corrected product requirement

The approved conceptual progression model is:

**Seven qualifying completed quests unlock one slightly more difficult mission.**

A mission is an actual playable challenge.

It is not an automatically awarded milestone.

After completing a mission, the user's ongoing progression continues.

There is no predefined final mission.

## 5.3 Preserve existing streak mechanics separately

Do not confuse:

- Quest completion count.
- Consecutive-day streak.
- Mission unlock progress.
- Level progression.
- Achievement milestones.
- Narrative chapter progress.

The mission cycle must not accidentally award seven-day streak bonuses.

Streak-based rewards should continue to follow their own established rules unless explicitly revised.

## 5.4 Audit the meaning of a qualifying quest

Before modifying production logic, inspect how Zynth currently represents:

- Daily quests.
- Calibration quests.
- Challenge quests.
- Hidden quests.
- Weakness quests.
- Prestige quests.
- Bonus quests.
- Missions.
- Swift Mode.
- Retests.
- Failed attempts.
- Skipped quests.

Do not assume every submission counts toward seven.

Build a precise eligibility policy based on the existing domain model.

Recommended initial policy:

- Count a quest only after an authoritative successful completion.
- Count each unique eligible quest once.
- Exclude mission completions from the seven-quest count.
- Exclude abandoned and rejected attempts.
- Do not count repeated grading or duplicate submissions.
- Define special quest eligibility explicitly rather than leaving it to accidental query behavior.

If the existing gameplay requires a different definition, document the conflict and implement the least disruptive interpretation.

## 5.5 Mission state machine

Introduce or refine explicit mission lifecycle states.

At minimum, distinguish:

- Not yet eligible.
- Progressing toward eligibility.
- Eligible.
- Mission generated.
- Mission available.
- Mission in progress.
- Mission completed.
- Mission failed or awaiting retry where supported.

Never confuse mission unlock with mission completion.

## 5.6 Repetition and cycle integrity

Ensure that:

- Seven completions do not unlock duplicate missions through repeated requests.
- A refresh does not generate a second copy of the same mission.
- Concurrent requests cannot double-award mission progress.
- Existing mission completions are preserved.
- A failed mission cannot silently be treated as completed.
- Another progression cycle can occur after a completed mission.

Define explicitly how additional completed quests are handled when a mission is pending.

Use a deterministic, documented policy.

## 5.7 Backward compatibility

Existing users may have mission history based on the original streak-triggered logic.

Do not reset their mission records or XP.

Do not retroactively fabricate completed missions.

Design a safe transition strategy.

If historical qualifying quest counts cannot be reconstructed reliably, document the limitation and select a conservative, nonpunitive migration approach.

## 5.8 Mission difficulty

The mission must be meaningfully harder than ordinary quests without becoming excessively demanding.

Retain adaptive personalization.

Use the existing Quest Generation and Adaptive Intelligence engines.

Do not introduce a new Mission Engine.

## 5.9 Mission verification

Add automated tests for:

- Exactly six qualifying quests.
- Seventh qualifying quest.
- Duplicate submission.
- Failed quest.
- Mission generation failure.
- Pending mission.
- Completed mission.
- Next cycle.
- Repeated API calls.
- Concurrent completion.
- Existing user migration.
- Streak independence.
- Prestige compatibility.

# 6. CHAPTERS ARE NOT THE SEVEN-QUEST CYCLE

This is a fundamental design requirement.

## 6.1 Gameplay hierarchy

Regular quests lead toward missions.

Missions are more challenging activities.

The cycle may repeat indefinitely.

## 6.2 Narrative hierarchy

Chapters are flexible narrative structures.

A chapter may contain:

- Multiple quests.
- Multiple missions.
- Significant achievements.
- Profession-related events.
- Discoveries.
- Player decisions.
- Environmental transformations.
- Narrative milestones.

Do not hardcode chapters to seven quests or seven missions.

## 6.3 Chapter completion

A chapter concludes when its configured narrative conditions are satisfied.

Those conditions must be deterministic and validated by the application.

A mission may trigger a chapter conclusion, but a mission is not automatically a chapter-ending event.

## 6.4 Open-ended chapter structure

Support:

- Main chapters.
- Side chapters.
- Optional narrative branches.
- Profession-influenced chapters.
- Future expansion chapters.
- Regional story arcs.

Start with the smallest coherent implementation.

Do not build a large narrative content management platform unnecessarily.
